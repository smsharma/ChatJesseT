What Jesse Thaler has been up to recently (summary as of September 2026, compiled from jthaler.net and MIT News).

Current roles: Jesse Thaler is the William and Emma Rogers Professor of Physics at MIT, in the MIT Center for Theoretical Physics - a Leinweber Institute (CTP-LI). On August 1, 2026, he became Director of the MIT Laboratory for Nuclear Science (LNS), succeeding Bolek Wyslouch, who directed LNS for the previous decade. LNS was established in 1946 to support nuclear and particle physics and now spans cosmology, gravity, field theory, and quantum information science. As head of LNS, Jesse also oversees his home center, CTP-LI. Asked why a tenured professor would take on this kind of administrative role, Jesse says: because discovery science is very much a team sport. He is excited to bring LNS into the next wave of discoveries supported by AI-driven capabilities, including through the Department of Energy's Genesis Mission.

IAIFI: Jesse was the inaugural Director of the NSF Institute for Artificial Intelligence and Fundamental Interactions (IAIFI) from 2020 to 2025. He is now the Former Director. Mike Williams, professor of physics at MIT, succeeded him as IAIFI Director. In June 2026, NSF renewed IAIFI for another five years with increased funding, and Boston University joined the institute alongside MIT, Harvard, Northeastern, and Tufts. Jesse describes IAIFI as a two-way street: AI enabling better physics, and physics enabling better AI.

Sabbatical: Jesse spent the 2025-2026 academic year on sabbatical in Europe, where he held the Schlumberger Chair for Mathematical Sciences at the Institut des Hautes Etudes Scientifiques (IHES) near Paris, France. During the sabbatical he gave the Chaire Georges Lemaitre Lectures at the Universite catholique de Louvain in Belgium (November 2025), lectured at the Galileo Galilei Institute (GGI) in Florence (January 2026), gave a colloquium at CERN (February 2026), and spoke at NIKHEF, IPhT Saclay, the Sorbonne Center for Artificial Intelligence, U. Geneva, Heidelberg (the Hans Jensen Lecture), Cambridge, Oxford, Paris-Saclay, Genova, LHCP 2026 in Paris, the Planetarium Hamburg, Rennes, Ecole Normale Superieure, and IRFU. He received a Humboldt Research Award from the Alexander von Humboldt Foundation in 2026. He returned to MIT in summer 2026 to start as LNS Director.

Talks and themes: His signature talk during this period is "Centaur Science: Adventures in AI+Physics", about humans and AI working together like a centaur, along with "Deep Learning + Deep Thinking = Deeper Understanding", "QCD Theory meets Information Theory", and "Descending into the Modular Bootstrap".

Recent research (2025-2026): using gradient descent and machine learning optimizers to explore the modular bootstrap for two-dimensional conformal field theories (Descending into the Modular Bootstrap); Sven, a singular value descent optimizer that acts as a computationally efficient natural gradient method; QCD Theory Meets Information Theory; improving parton shower predictions with precision moments of energy flow polynomials; unbinned unfolding with reweighting adversarial networks and moment unfolding; honest confidence intervals for misspecified models (The Well-Tempered Likelihood); simplex demixing to disentangle multiple light-flavor jets; leveraging biased simulations for unbiased parameter inference (Many Wrongs Make a Right); and a perspective on interpretability and explainability of machine learning in physics. He also co-led the community white paper The Future of Artificial Intelligence and the Mathematical and Physical Sciences (AI+MPS).

P5: Jesse served on the 2023 Particle Physics Project Prioritization Panel (P5). The panel finished its work and released its report, Exploring the Quantum Universe: Pathways to Innovation and Discovery in Particle Physics, in December 2023. The report set priorities for U.S. particle physics for the next 10 years within a 20-year context. Jesse is no longer serving on P5 because the panel has completed its charge.

Research group in 2026: postdocs Inbar Savoray, Kevin Langhoff, Xiaoyuan Zhang, and So Chigusa; Ph.D. students Pieter Van Steenweghen, Gregorio de la Fuente Simarro, and Pamela Pajarillo.
